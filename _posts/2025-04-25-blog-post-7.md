## Overview

In this project, I transferred a previously trained PPO reaching policy from simulation to a physical Universal Robots UR5e robot arm.

The policy had originally been trained in Isaac Lab and was subsequently validated through a Sim-to-Sim transfer, where it controlled a virtual UR5e through the ROS2 Universal Robots driver.

The final step was to replace the simulated robot with the physical UR5e while keeping the same policy and ROS2-based deployment pipeline. This allowed the complete workflow from reinforcement learning in simulation to execution on real robotic hardware to be demonstrated.


## Previous Steps

The experiment builds directly on two previous projects:
1. PPO Reach Policy Training
   - The UR5e reaching policy was trained in Isaac Lab using PPO.
   - The policy learned to move the robot's end-effector toward a target position.
2. Sim2Sim Transfer
   - The trained policy was deployed outside Isaac Lab.
   - ROS2 was used to exchange observations and actions with a virtual UR5e.
   - The virtual robot was controlled through the Universal Robots ROS2 driver.

The Sim2Real experiment uses the same deployment architecture, but replaces the virtual UR5e with the physical robot.


## Tools and Frameworks Used

- **Ubuntu 22.04**
- **ROS2 Humble**
- **Isaac Lab**
- **skrl PPO implementation**
- **UR5e robot**

## System Architecture

The deployment pipeline can be summarized as:

Isaac Lab PPO Training → Trained Policy → ROS2 Inference Pipeline → UR ROS2 Driver → Physical UR5e

During deployment, ROS2 provides the interface between the trained policy and the physical robot.

The policy receives the required robot state information through ROS2, constructs the observation vector expected by the neural network, calculates the next action, and sends the resulting command back to the UR5e through the robot driver.

This architecture is the same as the one previously validated during the Sim-to-Sim experiment.


## Implementation Steps

1. Using the Previously Trained Policy:
   - Reused the PPO reach policy previously trained in Isaac Lab.
   - Loaded the trained policy for inference without additional training.
2. Reusing the Sim2Sim Deployment Pipeline:
   - Used the same ROS2-based inference program developed for the Sim2Sim experiment.
   - Robot observations were recieved through ROS2.
   - Policy actions were sent back through the same ROS2 control interface.
3. Connecting the Real UR5e:
   - Replaced the virtual UR5e controller used during Sim2Sim testing with the real UR5e.
   - Connected the ROS2 workstation to the robot through the network.
   - Configured the Universal Robots ROS2 driver to communicate directly with the physical robot controller.
4. Launching the UR5e Driver:
   - Started the Universal Robots ROS2 driver using the IP address of the robot:
   ```bash
   ros2 launch ur_robot_driver ur_control.launch.py ur_type:=ur5e robot_ip:=192.168.56.101
   ```
5. Running the Policy:
   - Started the same inference program previously used for the Sim2Sim experiment:
   ```bash
   pyhton3 run_task.py
   ```
   - The program continously:
      - recieves observations from the robot,
      - constructs the policy input,
      - evaluates the trained PPO policy,
      - and sends the resulting actions to the UR5e.

6. Real-Robot Evaluation:
   - Executed the reaching task on the physical UR5e.
   - Observed the behavior of the policy outside the Isaac Lab simulation environment.
   - Verified that the complete ROS2-based control pipeline could be transferred from the simulated UR5e to the real robot.

The implementation was based on and adapted from:

[Louislelay/Isaaclab_Sim2Real](https://github.com/louislelay/isaaclab_ur_reach_sim2real)

## Outcome

The experiment successfully completed the full pipeline from reinforcement learning in simulation to policy execution on a physical robot.

A key aspect of the experiment was that the deployment architecture previously tested during the Sim-to-Sim transfer could also be used with the real UR5e. Instead of communicating with a virtual UR controller, the Universal Robots ROS2 driver communicated directly with the physical robot.

The resulting workflow was:

PPO Training in Isaac Lab → Sim-to-Sim Validation → Sim-to-Real Deployment

This experiment provides a foundation for further research into more complex Sim-to-Real robotic manipulation tasks and reinforcement learning policies.
